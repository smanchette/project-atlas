## 1. Purpose

This Blueprint defines the permanent principles by which Atlas plans, creates, reviews, publishes, operates, and improves complete business websites. It translates the Project Atlas Constitution into a website-specific operating model without prescribing a particular user interface, software framework, content-management system, hosting provider, or implementation technique.

A complete Atlas website is a coherent business asset. It combines verified business identity, useful content, purposeful presentation, approved media, trustworthy structured knowledge, controlled publication, and ongoing stewardship. A collection of unrelated pages is not a complete website.

Flo-Zone Pest And Termite Solutions Inc. is the reference implementation used to prove this model. Its facts, services, markets, brand, media, and publishing configuration are company-specific. The capabilities and rules that govern those inputs are reusable Atlas capabilities.

## 3. Atlas Website Philosophy

Atlas builds websites that people can use, businesses can own, operators can maintain, search engines can interpret, and AI systems can understand without sacrificing truth or human control.

Every website should express one business clearly:

- who the business is;
- what it legitimately offers;
- where it legitimately operates;
- why a customer should trust it;
- how a customer can take the next appropriate action; and
- how each published claim can be supported, reviewed, corrected, or retired.

Atlas favors clarity over novelty, usefulness over volume, evidence over assumption, coherent systems over isolated pages, and long-term stewardship over one-time publication. Automation may accelerate work, but it must not erase provenance, approval, accountability, or recovery.

Flo-Zone demonstrates this philosophy through a complete pest-control website grounded in verified company facts and legitimate service coverage. Atlas must be able to apply the same philosophy to another business without copying Flo-Zone's facts, voice, layout, or market structure.

## 4. Core Principles

### 4.1 Complete before expansive

Atlas should first produce a coherent, review-ready website with the pages, navigation, identity, media, conversion paths, and trust information required by the business. Expansion should strengthen that system rather than create disconnected volume.

### 4.2 Facts before content

Business claims, service descriptions, geographic coverage, qualifications, contact details, and other material statements must originate from approved knowledge or clearly identified research. Content must not become a substitute for fact management.

### 4.3 Separation of concerns

Atlas must preserve distinct domains for:

| Domain | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Business facts | Verified identity, services, locations, qualifications, policies, and approved claims |
| Content | Audience-facing language, page purpose, information structure, and calls to action |
| Presentation | Layout, components, typography, color, spacing, responsive behavior, and theme selection |
| Media | Images, logos, icons, video, documents, provenance, rights, and derivatives |
| Intelligence | External signals, observations, analytics, trends, recommendations, and confidence |
| Operational data | Customers, jobs, scheduling, dispatch, private records, and service operations |

These domains may inform one another through explicit interfaces. They must not be silently merged. In particular, intelligence is not automatically a business fact, presentation must not alter factual meaning, and private operational data must not become public website content without authorization.

### 4.4 Reusable capability, company-specific configuration

Atlas capabilities must be reusable across companies, sites, industries, and design systems. Company facts, brand assets, provider selections, site architecture, content, and approved exceptions remain configuration within the relevant company and website boundary.

### 4.5 Human control at consequential gates

Site architecture, business facts, media, material claims, publication, and significant production changes require appropriate human review. Atlas should explain unresolved issues and the consequences of approval.

### 4.6 Verifiable, recoverable operation

Important actions must be scoped, auditable, observable, and recoverable. The completed website must be verifiable as a public experience, not merely as stored records.

### 4.7 Accessible and portable by design

Websites should support accessible use, responsive presentation, durable semantics, and customer ownership of data and assets. Atlas must not depend on artificial lock-in.

## 5. Complete Website Lifecycle

Atlas uses a lifecycle rather than a generate-and-publish event.

1. **Establish context.** Identify the organization, company, brand, website, users, publishing destination, business goals, and approved scope.
2. **Build verified knowledge.** Gather business facts, sources, services, markets, qualifications, customer needs, constraints, and unresolved questions.
3. **Plan the website.** Define audiences, journeys, page inventory, navigation, conversion paths, internal relationships, media needs, and success criteria.
4. **Define identity and presentation.** Approve brand assets, website identity, design tokens, components, layouts, and responsive behavior.
5. **Produce content and media.** Create grounded briefs, drafts, metadata, structured knowledge, image plans, and approved media derivatives.
6. **Review and validate.** Apply factual, editorial, brand, accessibility, visual, media, SEO, structured-data, duplication, and technical quality gates.
7. **Preview and approve.** Present the complete site in context. Record decisions, exceptions, and required corrections.
8. **Protect and deploy.** Verify identity and backups, scope the change, publish through controlled operations, and preserve an audit trail.
9. **Verify the public result.** Confirm page identity, visible content, navigation, media, metadata, structured data, responsive presentation, and intended conversion paths.
10. **Operate and improve.** Monitor health, accuracy, freshness, performance, coverage, customer questions, and approved intelligence signals.
11. **Retire or replace safely.** Preserve history and redirects where appropriate, avoid orphaned experiences, and remove obsolete material through controlled change.

The lifecycle may be implemented incrementally. Its boundaries and responsibilities remain permanent even when a current milestone supports only part of the flow.

## 6. Business Identity

Business Identity is the authoritative, presentation-neutral description of the business. It may include legal and public names, brands, contact channels, services, service areas, locations, licenses, qualifications, operating policies, trust evidence, and approved claims.

Business Identity must:

- distinguish verified facts from claims, assumptions, and unresolved questions;
- record provenance and effective dates where material;
- support more than one brand or website without leaking identity between them;
- permit controlled reuse across pages without copying facts into unrelated content records;
- preserve customer ownership and exportability; and
- require approval before material public use.

For Flo-Zone, the company name, drywood-termite tenting services, legitimate Florida service areas, contact details, and verified qualifications are configuration values. Atlas owns the reusable identity model, validation, approval, and reuse capability.

Business Identity is not the same as visual brand identity. A logo may change without changing the legal business, and a content revision may change wording without changing an approved fact.

## 7. Brand Assets Manager

The Brand Assets Manager governs reusable visual assets associated with a company, brand, or website. It establishes support for:

- logo management;
- primary, alternate, monochrome, compact, and other approved logo variants;
- responsive logo placement;
- controlled logo sizing and clear-space rules;
- light, dark, and context-appropriate treatments;
- asset provenance, ownership, approval, versioning, and replacement;
- accessible text alternatives where applicable; and
- assignment of approved assets to one or more website identities.

Logo placement and sizing are presentation decisions, not destructive edits to the source asset. A theme or layout may request a suitable variant; it must not silently create a new brand mark.

For Flo-Zone, its approved logo files, variants, minimum sizes, placement rules, and brand colors are Flo-Zone configuration. The asset lifecycle, variant selection rules, responsive behavior, and approval model are reusable Atlas capabilities.

Brand assets must remain independently replaceable. A company should be able to update its logo or add an approved variant without rewriting site content or changing the underlying business facts.

---

## 8. Website Identity Manager

The Website Identity Manager governs the compact visual and machine-readable identity of an individual website. It establishes support for:

- favicon management;
- browser and shortcut icons;
- Apple Touch icons;
- future Progressive Web App icon sets;
- Open Graph identity images;
- site-name and brand associations;
- responsive and platform-appropriate variants;
- file-format and dimension validation; and
- versioned publication and verification.

Website identity is related to, but distinct from, business identity and page media. One company may operate multiple websites with different site identities while retaining the same verified business facts.

For Flo-Zone, the selected favicon, icon family, Open Graph identity asset, site name, and domain associations are configuration. Atlas provides the reusable validation, mapping, publication, fallback, and verification model.

No identity asset should be inferred to be approved merely because it exists in a media library. Selection and publication remain explicit.

## 9. Component Architecture

Atlas websites should be composed from semantic, reusable components governed by a theme architecture. The architecture establishes support for:

- **design tokens** for color, typography, spacing, sizing, borders, elevation, motion, and responsive behavior;
- a **component registry** describing available components, purposes, variants, content requirements, accessibility obligations, and compatible contexts;
- **theme adapters** that map common Atlas concepts to a specific rendering environment;
- **replaceable layouts** that organize components without owning business facts or content; and
- **multiple design systems** so companies are not forced into one visual template.

Components should express stable purposes such as navigation, service summaries, trust evidence, calls to action, location coverage, frequently asked questions, contact pathways, and media presentation. They should not embed Flo-Zone-specific facts in reusable definitions.

A component receives approved content, identity, and media through explicit bindings. Changing a theme should not rewrite facts. Changing content should not require modifying component source. Changing media should not alter page purpose.

Variation should be deliberate. Atlas may reuse a component model across sites while producing materially different visual systems and page compositions.

## 10. Page Architecture

Each page should have a defined role in the complete website. Its architecture should identify:

- audience and search intent;
- primary user need;
- approved business facts and claims;
- relationship to services and service areas;
- required components and content sections;
- conversion purpose and next actions;
- media plan;
- metadata and structured-knowledge responsibilities;
- navigation and internal-link relationships;
- ownership, approval, publication, and maintenance state; and
- duplication or cannibalization risk.

Common page types may include home, about, contact, service, county, city, service-by-location, informational, frequently asked questions, policies, and supporting resources. Page types are reusable planning structures, not rigid text templates.

Local pages must serve a legitimate user purpose. Geographic substitution alone is not sufficient. Atlas should avoid doorway-page patterns, invented local details, and repetitive content that adds no local value.

For Flo-Zone, the approved service and geographic relationships determine which pages are legitimate. Atlas provides page planning, relationship management, coverage analysis, content grounding, and quality rules.

Page architecture should support controlled replacement and consolidation. URLs, redirects, internal links, and historical identity must be considered when pages change.

## 11. Media and Image System

The Image System plans, creates, approves, transforms, publishes, and maintains images as governed website assets. It establishes support for:

- configurable image planning by page type, length, subject, audience, and available assets;
- approximately three to five images for substantial pages when appropriate;
- fewer or no images when additional imagery would not improve understanding, trust, or usability;
- AI image briefs that state purpose, subject, composition, brand constraints, prohibited details, and intended placement;
- uploaded, licensed, commissioned, and AI-generated media;
- explicit human approval;
- provenance, rights, source, creation method, and permitted-use records;
- featured, inline, social, thumbnail, and responsive derivatives;
- dimension, crop, format, compression, and responsive-delivery rules;
- meaningful alt text and decorative-image classification;
- duplicate and near-duplicate detection; and
- post-publication verification.

Image quantity is a planning guideline, not a quota. The page's purpose determines the media requirement.

Atlas must never fabricate GPS metadata. Verified GPS data may be preserved only for legitimate company photographs when the data is authentic, relevant, privacy-safe, and explicitly authorized. Generated images, stock images, screenshots, or transformed assets must not be presented as geotagged proof of a business location or service event.

For Flo-Zone, approved field photographs, equipment images, team assets, termite-related illustrations, service-area visuals, and brand treatments are company-specific. Atlas supplies the reusable planning, provenance, approval, transformation, accessibility, and delivery model.

Media must remain separate from page content so an approved asset can be replaced, retired, or reformatted without rewriting the page's factual meaning.

## 12. Navigation and User Experience

Navigation should reveal the website's structure and help visitors complete meaningful tasks. It should be derived from page relationships and user journeys, not merely from the order in which pages were created.

Atlas should support:

- clear global, local, utility, footer, and contextual navigation;
- responsive and keyboard-operable interactions;
- understandable labels and predictable hierarchy;
- direct contact and conversion paths;
- service and service-area discovery;
- breadcrumbs and related-content paths where useful;
- intentional internal links;
- visible focus, readable contrast, and semantic structure;
- graceful handling of unavailable optional features; and
- verification across representative devices and viewport sizes.

Customer portals are optional capabilities. Atlas should support provider adapters, including GorillaDesk, future providers, and AtlasOps360. A portal may be exposed only when enabled, configured, authorized, and verified. When disabled, it must be hidden completely: no empty navigation item, broken component, placeholder route, provider leakage, or implied availability.

For Flo-Zone, navigation labels, priority calls to action, service groupings, geographic groupings, contact routes, and any selected portal provider are configuration. Atlas governs the reusable interaction and visibility rules.

## 13. SEO, AI Discovery, and Structured Knowledge

Atlas should make accurate business knowledge understandable to people, conventional search engines, and emerging AI discovery systems. It should not optimize one channel by weakening usefulness or truth.

The website model should support:

- descriptive titles and summaries;
- canonical identity;
- semantic headings and content structure;
- legitimate local coverage;
- crawlable navigation and internal relationships;
- approved structured data;
- consistent business identity;
- media identity and alternatives;
- question-and-answer content grounded in real customer needs;
- source-aware facts and claims;
- duplicate, thin-content, and cannibalization controls; and
- verification of rendered public output.

Structured knowledge must be derived from approved facts and page meaning. It must not introduce claims that are absent, unsupported, expired, or prohibited in visible content.

AI discovery is an audience and interpretation concern, not permission to create speculative content. Atlas should favor clear entities, relationships, provenance, stable terminology, useful explanations, and consistent identity over channel-specific tricks.

For Flo-Zone, service names, geographic relationships, qualifications, frequently asked questions, and structured-data values are configuration based on approved knowledge. Atlas owns the reusable planning and verification capability.

## 14. Future Intelligence Architecture

Atlas should support an extensible intelligence layer that observes external signals without confusing them with durable business truth. Provider adapters may eventually supply:

- Google search trends;
- Bing and Yahoo search trends;
- AI discovery and citation observations;
- website health and technical-quality signals;
- competitor observations;
- analytics;
- Search Console data;
- review monitoring;
- customer questions; and
- selected AtlasOps360 operational insights.

The provider-adapter architecture should normalize access while preserving provider identity, source time, scope, confidence, limitations, and raw-versus-derived distinctions. An adapter may be replaced without changing the business facts, content model, or website presentation.

Intelligence can identify opportunities, risks, questions, and maintenance needs. It cannot silently:

- become an approved company fact;
- authorize a public claim;
- rewrite or publish content;
- expose private operational data;
- override human approval; or
- merge one company's observations into another company's private context.

For Flo-Zone, selected providers, connected accounts, monitored competitors, target markets, review sources, and approved operational signals are configuration. Atlas supplies the reusable adapter, classification, governance, privacy, and recommendation boundaries.

## 15. Quality Assurance and Human Review

Quality assurance applies to the whole website and to each governed domain. Appropriate checks include:

- fact and claim verification;
- source and provenance review;
- company, brand, contact, service, and geography consistency;
- content usefulness, readability, originality, and intent;
- duplicate and cannibalization analysis;
- page relationships, navigation, internal links, and conversion paths;
- logo, icon, media, rights, alt text, crops, and responsive delivery;
- layout, accessibility, responsive behavior, and visual regression;
- titles, canonical identity, metadata, and structured data;
- security, privacy, authentication boundaries, and secret isolation;
- backups, deployment scope, audit records, recovery readiness, and public verification; and
- site-wide completeness against the approved plan.

Automated checks should be deterministic where possible and explain failures in operator-readable terms. Human review is required where judgment, brand suitability, factual interpretation, image appropriateness, legal sensitivity, or publication consequences cannot be safely reduced to a mechanical rule.

Approval of one domain does not imply approval of every domain. An approved image is not automatically approved for every page; an approved fact is not automatically approved in every claim; a technically valid page is not automatically ready to publish.

Flo-Zone serves as the first end-to-end quality reference. Its results should improve reusable Atlas checks without turning Flo-Zone-specific preferences into universal rules.

Flo-Zone Pest And Termite Solutions Inc. is Atlas's first complete-website proving ground. It validates the lifecycle with a real business, real publishing environment, real service geography, and real operational consequences.

The following distinction is permanent:

| Reusable Atlas capability | Flo-Zone-specific configuration |
| --- | --- |
| Business Identity model and approvals | Flo-Zone's approved name, contacts, qualifications, services, and service areas |
| Brand Assets Manager | Flo-Zone logos, variants, colors, and usage rules |
| Website Identity Manager | Flo-Zone favicon, icon family, site identity, and social identity asset |

Lessons from Flo-Zone should become reusable capabilities only when they generalize cleanly. Flo-Zone facts must never become defaults for another company. Flo-Zone's current theme, page count, WordPress structure, provider choices, or operational workflows must not become architectural requirements.

The reference implementation is successful when it proves that Atlas can deliver a complete, accurate, coherent, visually intentional, review-ready, deployable, and maintainable business website—and when the same system can then serve a second company through configuration rather than a core rewrite.

## 17. Blueprint Rules

The following rules govern complete Atlas websites:

1. The Constitution always takes precedence.
2. A website must be planned and evaluated as a coherent system, not as a page count.
3. Business facts, content, presentation, media, intelligence, and operational data must remain distinct.
4. Public content and structured knowledge must be grounded in approved facts and claims.
5. Reusable capabilities must not contain Flo-Zone-specific facts or assumptions.
6. Company and website boundaries must be explicit.
7. Themes, layouts, components, identities, media, and providers must be replaceable through governed interfaces.
8. Optional features must be absent from the public experience when disabled.
9. Images require purpose, provenance, approval, and appropriate responsive treatment.
10. GPS metadata must never be fabricated.
11. Local coverage must provide legitimate user value and avoid doorway-page behavior.
12. Intelligence remains an attributed signal until separately promoted through a governed knowledge process.
13. Consequential production changes require scoped authorization, verification, auditability, and recovery.
14. Human review remains mandatory where automation cannot establish truth or suitability.
15. A complete website must remain ownable, portable, maintainable, and understandable by future stewards.
16. Deferred implementation does not erase an architectural capability established here.

## 18. Atlas Website Operating Model

Atlas operates a website through related but independently governed planes:

- **Knowledge plane:** business facts, research, claims, assumptions, provenance, and approvals.
- **Experience plane:** content, page relationships, navigation, components, themes, accessibility, and conversion paths.
- **Media plane:** brand assets, website identity, images, derivatives, rights, provenance, and delivery.
- **Intelligence plane:** trends, analytics, health, competitors, reviews, questions, and recommendations.
- **Operations plane:** publishing destinations, credentials, backups, deployments, audits, verification, recovery, and maintenance.
- **Business-operations boundary:** customer and job data owned by operational systems such as GorillaDesk or AtlasOps360 and exposed to Atlas only through approved adapters.

Each plane has its own state, approvals, and change history. Cross-plane actions must be explicit. For example, an intelligence signal may open a content-review task, but it must not directly publish a claim. A theme may request a logo variant, but it must not rewrite the Brand Asset record. A customer portal may provide an authenticated entry point, but its private data must not enter public-page generation.

The operator should be able to understand:

- what is approved;
- what is proposed;
- what is missing or uncertain;
- which company and site are affected;
- which domain owns a value;
- what will change;
- what evidence and backup protect the action; and
- how to verify or recover afterward.

This model supports manual operation today and progressively automated operation later without weakening responsibility.

This Blueprint begins at version 1.0 and is a governing design document subordinate to the Project Atlas Constitution.

1. review of the Constitution, Architecture, Roadmap, and current Blueprint;
2. identification of the affected website domains and lifecycle stages;

Any proposed conflict with the Constitution must be resolved in favor of the Constitution unless the Constitution itself is separately and explicitly amended.

## 20. Summary

Atlas builds complete business websites by combining verified business identity, approved knowledge, purposeful content, adaptable presentation, governed media, structured discovery, human review, safe publication, and long-term operation.

The system is reusable; the company configuration is specific. Flo-Zone proves the complete model but does not define the limits of Atlas. Facts, content, presentation, media, intelligence, and operational data remain separate so each can evolve without corrupting the others.

The lasting standard is not how quickly Atlas can produce pages. It is whether a business can confidently rely on the complete website Atlas creates and maintains.

## Appendix A: Atlas Design Principles

1. **Truth before output.** Verified knowledge is the foundation of public content.
2. **Whole-site coherence.** Pages, navigation, media, identity, and conversion paths form one system.
3. **Configuration over hard-coding.** Company differences belong in governed configuration.
4. **Separation over coupling.** Facts, content, presentation, media, intelligence, and operations evolve independently.
5. **Semantics before decoration.** Meaning and accessibility precede visual treatment.
6. **Purposeful media.** Every image should improve understanding, trust, identity, or usability.
7. **Controlled variation.** Reuse should create quality and efficiency without making sites identical.
8. **Optional means absent when off.** Disabled capabilities leave no broken or misleading public trace.
9. **Evidence before automation.** Important decisions and mutations require verifiable inputs and results.
10. **Human judgment where it matters.** Automation supports rather than conceals consequential decisions.
11. **Recovery is part of delivery.** Backup, audit, verification, and recovery belong to the website lifecycle.
12. **Portability protects the customer.** Business data and website assets remain ownable and exportable.
13. **Signals are not facts.** Intelligence retains source, time, scope, confidence, and limitations.
14. **Design for future stewards.** The system and its outputs must remain understandable and maintainable.

## Appendix B: Glossary

**Atlas capability**  
A reusable platform function that can serve multiple companies, websites, industries, or providers without embedding one customer's facts.

**Business Identity**  
The approved, presentation-neutral record of who a business is, what it offers, where it operates, and which material claims it may make.

**Brand asset**  
An approved visual asset associated with a company or brand, including logos and governed variants.

**Complete website**  
A coherent, reviewable, deployable, and maintainable business asset whose identity, pages, navigation, content, media, discovery signals, conversion paths, and operating controls work together.

**Component**  
A reusable semantic presentation unit with a defined purpose, inputs, variants, and accessibility obligations.

**Configuration**  
Company-, brand-, website-, market-, provider-, or theme-specific values supplied to reusable Atlas capabilities.

**Design system**  
A governed set of tokens, components, interaction patterns, layout rules, and visual conventions.

**Design token**  
A named presentation value such as color, typography, spacing, sizing, border, elevation, motion, or breakpoint behavior.

**Intelligence**  
Attributed observations and derived signals used to inform decisions; intelligence is not an approved business fact by default.

**Media provenance**  
The recorded origin, ownership, creation method, rights, transformations, approvals, and permitted use of a media asset.

**Operational data**  
Private information used to operate the business, including customers, jobs, scheduling, dispatch, and service records.

**Provider adapter**  
A bounded interface that connects Atlas to an external provider while preserving provider identity, source limitations, and Atlas domain boundaries.

**Structured knowledge**  
Approved entities, facts, claims, and relationships expressed in machine-readable form without changing their factual status.

**Theme adapter**  
A mapping between Atlas's stable component and content concepts and a particular rendering or design environment.

**Website Identity**  
The site-specific visual and machine-readable identity represented by favicons, browser icons, touch icons, social identity assets, and related associations.
